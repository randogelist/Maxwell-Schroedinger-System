CENTRAL IMPROVEMENT
-------------------
A previous sufficient theorem switched 3D forcing ON AFTER a positive
entry time t0>0 to accommodate rough L2 2D3C cores.

For SMOOTH large helical cores, there is NO NEED for a positive delay:
small reciprocal graph coupling may act for all t>=0.

Three independent spatial directions are explicitly mixed into each
layer at first order in t, and the NSE solenoidal quadratic nonlinearity
becomes genuinely nonzero. All smooth finite-energy amplitudes are
allowed, provided the nonzero exchange conductance is sufficiently
small relative to the reference amplitude.

This is a nontrivial rank-3, nonlinear, globally regular sector of
FINITE-LAYER NSE^X, not a proof for arbitrary 3D NSE data.
The coupling represents a mathematical reciprocal fluid drag, NOT
an already derived physical electron/Coulomb/SM interaction.

0. NORMALIZATION
----------------
M=T_L^3, L>0, volume V=L^3. Fix viscosity nu>0, the same in
each layer; all densities are 1 (or absorbed into time and force
coefficients). Work with real, smooth, periodic mean-zero
solenoidal fields and the homogeneous Sobolev norms defined
through Fourier frequencies (2 pi/L) Z^3.

Let
  A=-P_sigma Delta,
  B(u,v)=P_sigma[(u dot grad)v],
  K_0 = [[2,-1,-1],[-1,2,-1],[-1,-1,2]],
  L_kappa=kappa K_0, kappa>=0.

The graph matrix is symmetric nonnegative, with eigenvalues
  {0,3,3} and operator norm lambda_G=3.

1. THREE ORTHOGONAL-PROPAGATION HELICAL BASE PROFILES
-----------------------------------------------------
Fix nonzero integer wave numbers n1,n2,n3, with
  k1=2 pi n1/L, k2=2 pi n2/L, k3=2 pi n3/L,
and choose k1 != k2; for a concrete rank-3 nonlinear test
use (n1,n2,n3)=(1,2,3).

Fix nonzero finite amplitudes A1,A2,A3 in R and phases
alpha1,alpha2,alpha3 in R.

Define
  a1(x)=A1 (0,cos(k1*x+alpha1),sin(k1*x+alpha1)),
  a2(y)=A2 (sin(k2*y+alpha2),0,cos(k2*y+alpha2)),
  a3(z)=A3 (cos(k3*z+alpha3),sin(k3*z+alpha3),0).

For each i:
  div ai=0,
  integral ai dx=0,
  Delta ai=-ki^2 ai,
  (ai dot grad)ai=0,
  curl ai=-ki ai.

Thus the UNCOUPLED exact NSE reference is
  Ui(t)=exp(-nu ki^2 t)ai,
and U=(U1,U2,U3) is a smooth globally regular product
NSE solution. No amplitude smallness on Ai is required.

2. IMMEDIATE GRAPH COUPLING
--------------------------
Consider the genuinely coupled NSE^3 system

  partial_t ui+B(ui,ui)+nu A ui
    +kappa sum_{j != i}(ui-uj)=0,      i=1,2,3,

  ui(0)=ai, div ui=0.

For any local smooth solution,
  partial_t u1(0)
    =-nu k1^2 a1-kappa(2a1-a2-a3).

Consequently, for kappa>0 and all amplitudes Aj!=0, the
Fourier coefficients of u1 at the x, y and z wave numbers
are nonzero at sufficiently small positive times:
  u1(t)
    =a1+t[-nu k1^2 a1-kappa(2a1-a2-a3)]+O(t^2)
in every smooth Sobolev space on a local existence
interval. The O(t^2) terms are smooth.

Therefore the individual first layer immediately acquires
genuine rank-3 Fourier dependence for t>0 small enough.
A common invariant 2D3C direction does NOT exist for the
combined three-axis family.

For general positive times, global rank-3 persistence of
each individual Fourier coefficient is not asserted;
the theorem only needs rank-3 nontriviality near t=0.

3. NONZERO SOLENOIDAL CROSS ADVECTION
-------------------------------------
Let
  hx(x)=(0,cos(k1*x+alpha1),sin(k1*x+alpha1)),
  hy(y)=(sin(k2*y+alpha2),0,cos(k2*y+alpha2)).

The symmetric convection cross-term is
  f12=(a1 dot grad)a2+(a2 dot grad)a1.

Its z component is
  (f12)_z
    =A1*A2*(k1-k2)
       cos(k1*x+alpha1)*sin(k2*y+alpha2).

As f12 depends only on x,y, the pressure projection
P_sigma modifies only the x,y components of its
Fourier modes: for x,y wave vectors, the projection
P_(kx,ky,0) leaves the z component unchanged.

Thus for k1 != k2 and A1*A2 !=0,

  P_sigma f12 !=0.

Expanding B(u1,u1) around a1 in the coupled solution
shows

  B(u1(t),u1(t))
   =kappa*t*[B(a1,a2)+B(a2,a1)
              +B(a1,a3)+B(a3,a1)]
       +O(t^2).

The contribution at Fourier mode (k1,k2,0) has a
nonzero z component above, while the a1/a3
cross-term has Fourier mode (k1,0,k3).
They cannot cancel.

Therefore
  B(u1(t),u1(t)) !=0
for all sufficiently small positive t.

This is real nonlinear NSE interaction in a
genuinely rank-3 layer, not a Beltrami cancellation.

4. PERTURBATION EQUATION ABOUT THE LARGE REGULAR PRODUCT
---------------------------------------------------------
Write
  ui=Ui+wi,
  w(0)=0.

The coupled equations become

  partial_t wi+nu A wi+(L_kappa w)i
   +B(Ui,wi)+B(wi,Ui)+B(wi,wi)
      =hi,

where the forcing is exactly
  h=-L_kappa U=-kappa K_0 U.

This is an algebraic decomposition of the AUTONOMOUS,
UNFORCED, COUPLED PDE; h is not an additional external force.

Define
  H_w(t)=sum_i||wi(t)||_(dot H^(1/2))^2,
  D_w(t)=sum_i||wi(t)||_(dot H^(3/2))^2,
  F(t)=sum_i||hi(t)||_(dot H^(-1/2))^2.

Let C_NL>0 be a valid 3D critical trilinear constant,
and C_mix>0 a valid mixed bilinear constant such that

  |<Lambda^(1/2)B(wi,wi),Lambda^(1/2)wi>|
    <=C_NL||wi||_(dot H^(1/2))
             ||wi||_(dot H^(3/2))^2,

  ||B(Ui,wi)+B(wi,Ui)||_(dot H^(-1/2))
    <= C_mix ||Ui||_(W^(2,infinity))
                   ||wi||_(dot H^(1/2)).

These are standard smooth-background Sobolev product
inequalities. Constants depend on the specified Fourier
normalization and torus geometry.

Let
  M_U(t)^2=sum_i||Ui(t)||_(W^(2,infinity))^2.

Since the positive graph commutes with spatial Fourier
multipliers, the critical energy inequality is

  (1/2) H_w' +nu D_w
     +kappa sum_{i<j}||wi-wj||_(dot H^(1/2))^2
  <= C_NL sqrt(H_w) D_w
     +C_mix M_U sqrt(H_w) sqrt(D_w)
     +sqrt(F) sqrt(D_w).

Fix
  delta=nu/(4C_NL).

On the bootstrap interval sqrt(H_w)<=delta, the
critical nonlinearity is <=nu/4 D_w.
Two Young estimates with budget nu/4 each give

  H_w' <=beta_U(t) H_w+(2/nu) F(t),

  beta_U(t)
     =(2C_mix^2/nu) M_U(t)^2.

Let
  B_U=int_0^infty beta_U(t)dt.

Each Ui is an exponentially decaying smooth single
Fourier eigenmode, hence B_U<infinity for EVERY
finite A1,A2,A3 and every nu>0.

Since H_w(0)=0, Gronwall yields

  sup_{t>=0}H_w(t)
    <=exp(B_U)*(2/nu)*int_0^infty F(t)dt

whenever the right side is strictly less than delta^2.
This closes the bootstrap and global critical strong
continuation. Smooth data and no external nonsmooth
forcing yield global smooth solutions on [0,infinity).

5. EXPLICIT GRAPH-COUPLING ADMISSION
-------------------------------------
Because K0 is a real symmetric 3x3 graph matrix with
  ||K0||_op=lambda_G=3,

and its action commutes with Lambda, one has

  ||h(t)||_(dot H^(-1/2),X)^2
   <=kappa^2*lambda_G^2
      sum_i ||Ui(t)||_(dot H^(-1/2))^2.

For the pure helical modes,

  ||Ui(t)||_(dot H^(-1/2))^2
   =V*Ai^2/|ki| *exp(-2nu ki^2 t).

Thus

  integral_0^infinity ||h(t)||_(dot H^(-1/2),X)^2 dt
   <=kappa^2*lambda_G^2/(2nu)*
      [V*sum_i Ai^2/|ki|^3].

Define the EXACT positive finite weighted spectral sum

  S_m3 :=V*sum_{i=1}^3 Ai^2/|ki|^3 >0.

A sufficient strict admissibility condition is

  exp(B_U)*
       [kappa^2*lambda_G^2*S_m3/nu^2]
    < nu^2/(16 C_NL^2).

Equivalently,

  0<kappa<kappa_crit,

  kappa_crit
    := nu^2/(4 C_NL*lambda_G*sqrt(S_m3))
       *exp(-B_U/2).

For the complete graph lambda_G=3:

  kappa_crit
    =nu^2/[12 C_NL sqrt(S_m3)]
       *exp(-B_U/2).

This is strictly positive for every fixed finite
amplitude vector Ai, nonzero wave numbers, and nu>0.

If E0=V*sum_i Ai^2 and k_min=min_i |ki|,
then S_m3 <=E0/k_min^3; a more conservative
sufficient parameter restriction is

  0<kappa<
  [nu^2*k_min^(3/2)/(12 C_NL sqrt(E0))]
    *exp(-B_U/2).

For a W^(2,infinity) convention with
||Ai h_i||_W2infty<=|Ai| C_i,
and C_i finite (bounded by suitable combinations
of 1,|ki|,|ki|^2), the explicit upper bound

  B_U <=(C_mix^2/nu^2)*
             sum_i Ai^2*C_i^2/ki^2

may be used in the threshold instead of integrating
the known exponential reference numerically.

6. ENERGY IDENTITY AND NONLINEAR 3D REGULARITY
-----------------------------------------------
For the actual coupled solution,

  E_X(t)=1/2 sum_i||ui(t)||_2^2,

  dE_X/dt
    =-nu sum_i||grad ui(t)||_2^2
     -kappa sum_{i<j}||ui(t)-uj(t)||_2^2
    <=0.

Thus the graph coupling is physically dissipative
at the level of the abstract multifluid model.
It preserves mean-zero total momentum and does not
supply energy to the nonlinear interaction.

The critical perturbation theorem shows smoothness
in spite of the rank-3 nonlinear Fourier transfer,
not because the quadratic NSE term vanishes.

By weak-strong uniqueness, the constructed smooth
solution is the unique Leray-Hopf trajectory from
the same smooth initial three-layer datum, under
the standard finite graph system energy inequality.

THEOREM:

For every nu>0, finite nonzero Ai, nonzero ki with
k1 != k2, and complete 3-vertex graph, there exists
a strict positive kappa_crit such that for all
0<kappa<kappa_crit the smooth coupled NSE^3
trajectory is GLOBAL, genuinely rank-3 for small
positive time in each layer and has nonzero projected
quadratic interaction for small positive time.

In particular, for arbitrarily large finite initial
energy E0, one may choose a sufficiently small BUT
STRICTLY POSITIVE kappa to preserve global regularity.
The allowed kappa generally shrinks as E0 increases;
the result does not say that any large kappa is safe
for arbitrary rank-3 interacting data.

7. RELATION TO ROUGH CORE AND STANDARD MODEL GATES
--------------------------------------------------
The previous rough L2 2D3C theorem requires t0>0
for completely uncontrolled 3D leakage. The present
instantaneous theorem avoids t0 by assuming the
uncoupled base U is already globally smooth at t=0,
which supplies the finite stability integral B_U(0).

It does NOT imply the same immediate coupling from
an arbitrary rough L2 2D3C core, because the near-zero
background W2infty stability integral may diverge.

The mathematical graph conductance kappa is an
interlayer viscous-exchange coefficient, NOT a
derived electromagnetic electron-electron scattering
coupling or Standard Model gauge parameter.

Attaching a physical field Lagrangian requires:
  (a) a gauge-covariant force/current/stress port,
  (b) conservation of total field/fluid/reservoir
      energy including residual electron currents,
  (c) a quantitative bound on the actual coupling's
      off-core H^(-1/2) size or a tangent cancellation,
  (d) a no-double-counting microscopic matching,
  (e) proper renormalization/quantum structure.

All are outside the present pure NSE^3 theorem.
