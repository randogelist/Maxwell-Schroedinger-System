PURPOSE
-------
Distinguish the interacting two-electron Hamiltonian from the kinetic
energy of the current-adapted NSE velocity. Construct exact finite
operator representations of both, identify energy-variance corrections,
derive the NSE energy/Koopman commutator, and isolate three independent
clock scales. Do not identify a classical NSE time parameter with a
self-adjoint quantum time operator.

The construction uses the frozen electron-current two-helix NSE data;
its smooth 2D3C global solution is already established at B-tier.

1. QUANTUM TWO-ELECTRON ENERGY OPERATOR
---------------------------------------
Let

  h := L2(T_L^3; C^2),
  H_2e := exterior_square h.

With electron mass m_e>0, charge -e, and external A=0, define formally
on the natural periodic Coulomb Hamiltonian form domain

  H_C := [-hbar^2/(2m_e)] (Delta_1 + Delta_2)
         + (e^2/epsilon_0) G_L(x_1-x_2),

where the periodic neutral-background Green kernel solves

  -Delta G_L=delta_0-1/L^3,  integral_{T_L^3} G_L=0.

The green kernel includes a declared background convention. Add Pauli
magnetic terms or a quantized electromagnetic field only if their
degrees of freedom have actually been modeled.

For a rigorous, entirely finite numerical endpoint, choose d
orthonormal one-electron Pauli orbitals {chi_1,...,chi_d}, project to

  H_d := exterior_square span{chi_1,...,chi_d},
  dim H_d = d(d-1)/2,

and define the Hermitian matrix

  H_d^C := P_d H_C P_d

with exact or numerically approximated matrix elements

  (H_d^C)_{IJ}=<chi_i wedge chi_j,H_C (chi_k wedge chi_l)>.

The finite matrix is self-adjoint without unbounded-operator domain
issues. Its energy and variance for normalized Psi in H_d are

  E_Q(Psi) := <Psi,H_d^C Psi>,

  Delta_H(Psi)^2
    := <Psi,(H_d^C)^2 Psi>-E_Q(Psi)^2.

This variance, not a classical kinetic-energy value and not merely
a difference of selected eigenvalues, governs the quantum energy-time
speed bound.

IMPORTANT MINIMUM DIMENSION:
  d=2 -> dim H_d=1; H_d^C acts as a scalar and Delta_H=0.
  d=3 -> dim H_d=3; nontrivial transitions can occur.
  d=4 -> dim H_d=6; recommended first two-electron interaction matrix.

An interaction expectation on a single Slater determinant is not the
same thing as nontrivial dynamics inside a one-dimensional truncation.

2. QUANTUM MANDELSTAM-TAMM TIME
-------------------------------
Under time-independent self-adjoint H_d^C, if A is a self-adjoint
time-independent observable, on states with nonzero derivative set

  tau_A(Psi_t)
    := Delta_{Psi_t} A / |d<E_A>_t/dt|.

The Heisenberg equation and Robertson inequality imply

  Delta_{Psi_t} H_d^C * tau_A(Psi_t) >= hbar/2.

This time depends on the chosen observable A and quantum state.
It is not a universal self-adjoint time operator, and it is not
T_QFB. In d=2 there is no nontrivial quantum clock in the truncated
two-electron antisymmetric space because H_d^C is a scalar.

3. QUANTUM CURRENT ENERGY: ONE-COPY VERSUS TWO-COPY OPERATORS
-------------------------------------------------------------
The mean-zero projected velocity defined from the electron number
current j_Psi and number density n_Psi is

  a(Psi) = P_{sigma,0}(j_Psi/n_Psi).

As a function of Psi this is nonlinear due to division by density.
Its NSE energy

  E_NSE[a(Psi)] = rho_f/2 * ||a(Psi)||_2^2

is not generally the expectation of one fixed linear operator on the
two-electron Hilbert space.

For an exact finite endpoint, freeze a spatially constant number
density n0>0 at the state under examination (the frozen two-orbital
state has n0=2/L^3). Fix a real orthonormal basis {e_mu}_{mu=1}^N
of mean-zero divergence-free vector fields. Let Jhat_mu be Hermitian
finite-matrix operators whose expectations are the coefficients of the
ONE-BODY total electron number current:

  J_mu(rho) = Tr(rho Jhat_mu)
            = integral <j(x)>_rho dot e_mu(x) dx.

Then the finite-mode current-adapted NSE velocity and its kinetic
energy are

  a_N(rho)=n0^(-1) sum_mu J_mu(rho) e_mu,

  E_cur,N(rho)=C sum_mu [J_mu(rho)]^2,
  C:=rho_f/(2 n0^2).

There are two DIFFERENT exact operatorizations.

(A) ONE-COPY POSITIVE SELF-ADJOINT OPERATOR:

  Ehat_J^(1)=C sum_mu (Jhat_mu)^2.

Its expectation satisfies

  Tr(rho Ehat_J^(1))
   =E_cur,N(rho) + C sum_mu Var_rho(Jhat_mu).

Therefore the classical squared-current energy is the expectation
of this positive operator MINUS a nonnegative quantum current
fluctuation correction.

(B) TWO-COPY SELF-ADJOINT OPERATOR:

  Ehat_J^(2)=C sum_mu Jhat_mu tensor Jhat_mu.

Then, on product copies of the same quantum density matrix,

  Tr[(rho tensor rho) Ehat_J^(2)] = E_cur,N(rho).

This is an exact representation of the nonlinear squared-mean
functional using two COPIES of the entire two-electron Hilbert
space. These copies are mathematical replicas, NOT two more
physical electrons. The operator is self-adjoint; it need not be
positive on arbitrary correlated states of the two copies.

The constant-density gate n_Psi=n0 is an initial-state gate for the
frozen helical construction, not a theorem that an interacting
Coulomb-evolved state preserves uniform density. A variable-density
adapter requires a separate regularization or quotient treatment.

4. CLASSICAL NSE ENERGY MULTIPLICATION OPERATOR
-----------------------------------------------
Fix a divergence-free real Fourier Galerkin space X_N with smooth
velocity u_N(z) parameterized by z in R^d. The unforced NSE Galerkin
ODE is

  zdot=b_N(z).

Define the physical kinetic-energy function

  E_N(z):=rho_f/2 ||u_N(z)||_2^2,

and dissipation function

  D_N(z):=rho_f*nu ||grad u_N(z)||_2^2.

The Galerkin cancellation of the incompressible quadratic convection
gives the exact identity

  DE_N(z)[b_N(z)] = -D_N(z).

On L2(X_N,mu) for a declared measure mu, multiplication by E_N

  (Ehat_N F)(z):=E_N(z)F(z)

is a self-adjoint nonnegative multiplication operator with domain

  {F in L2(mu): E_N F in L2(mu)}.

On differentiable test observables define the first-order Koopman
(Liouville) generator

  L_N F := DF(z)[b_N(z)].

Then the exact COMMUTATOR IDENTITY is

  [L_N,Ehat_N]F
    = (L_N E_N)F
    = -D_N(z)F.

Hence

  [L_N,Ehat_N] = -Dhat_N

on the common test-function domain.

WARNING: L_N need not be self-adjoint, and this is NOT
[X,P]=i hbar I or [T,H]=i hbar I. It is the operator form of
deterministic viscous dissipation. The Hilbert space of classical
Galerkin ensemble observables differs from the two-electron
quantum Hilbert space.

5. NONLINEAR NSE MULTICLOCK SCALES
----------------------------------
For a nonzero smooth mean-zero NSE trajectory set

  E(t):=rho_f/2 ||u(t)||_2^2,
  D(t):=rho_f*nu ||grad u(t)||_2^2.

Then

  Edot(t)=-D(t).

(A) INSTANTANEOUS ENERGY DISSIPATION TIME:

  tau_D(t):=E(t)/D(t),

when D(t)>0.

(B) INSTANTANEOUS NONLINEAR TRANSFER TIME:

  tau_NL(t):=||u(t)||_2
            /||P_sigma[(u(t) dot grad)u(t)]||_2,

when denominator>0; otherwise define tau_NL=+infinity.

(C) FROZEN QUASI-FEFFERMAN-B CERTIFICATE TIME:

  T_QFB(a,nu):=||a||_2^4/(8pi^2 nu^5)
              =E(0)^2/(2pi^2 rho_f^2 nu^5).

This is a sufficient regularity waiting/certification bound
and not a physical or quantum uncertainty time.

(D) QUANTUM CHARACTERISTIC TIME:

  tau_A(Psi) >= hbar/(2 Delta_H(Psi)).

No equality between A, B, C, D follows from the current adapter.

(E) ENERGY THRESHOLD HITTING TIME:

  T_{E*}(a):=inf{t>=0 : E[u(t)]<=E*},

for E*>0. For a SINGLE circular Laplace-eigenmode,
E(t)=E(0) exp(-2nu k^2t) and

  T_{E*}(a)=max(0, ln(E(0)/E*)/(2nu k^2)).

For interacting 2D3C profiles energy transfer makes this
generally nonexponential. This is a deterministic hitting time,
not a canonical self-adjoint time observable.

Because the operator Ehat_N is multiplication, so is the optional

  That_QFB:=(1/(2pi^2 rho_f^2 nu^5)) Ehat_N^2.

Thus [Ehat_N,That_QFB]=0: operatorizing the QFB cutoff by itself
does NOT create a Heisenberg energy-time commutator.

6. EXACT INITIAL TIMES FOR THE TWO-HELIX ELECTRON ADAPTER
---------------------------------------------------------
Use the frozen two-electron current datum

  a(x,z)=A s_1(z)+B s_2(x),

  s_1=(cos(kz+alpha),sin(kz+alpha),0),
  s_2=(0,cos(ell x+beta),sin(ell x+beta)),

  A=-hbar k/(4m_e), B=-hbar ell/(4m_e).

The input is y-independent, so the global 2D3C NSE solution is
smooth for all t>=0.

At t=0,

  ||a||_2^2=V(A^2+B^2),

  ||grad a||_2^2=V(k^2 A^2+ell^2 B^2),

  tau_D(0)
    =(A^2+B^2)/(2nu(k^2 A^2+ell^2 B^2))
    =(k^2+ell^2)/(2nu(k^4+ell^4)).

The solenoidal cross-interaction has exact norm

  ||P_sigma[(a dot grad)a]||_2^2
    = V A^2 B^2/4 *
       [ (ell^2-k^2)^2/(k^2+ell^2) + (k-ell)^2 ].

Hence, if k != ell,

  tau_NL(0)
   =2 sqrt(A^2+B^2)/(
        |AB| sqrt((ell^2-k^2)^2/(k^2+ell^2)+(k-ell)^2)
     ),

and if k=ell, tau_NL(0)=infinity.

The QFB certificate is

  T_QFB
    = V^2 (A^2+B^2)^2/(8pi^2 nu^5)
    = L^6 hbar^4(k^2+ell^2)^2
      /(2048pi^2 m_e^4 nu^5).

All three quantities are computable from (L,n,m,nu),
but generally differ. In this 2D3C regime the actual NSE
regularity gap is ZERO despite T_QFB>0.

7. FINITE-DIMENSIONAL CLOSED-CLOCK NO-GO
----------------------------------------
Suppose H is a time-independent Hermitian matrix on a finite quantum
Hilbert space, A is a bounded self-adjoint observable, and Psi_t
=exp(-iHt/hbar) Psi_0. Then

  <A>_t
    =sum_{i,j} c_i* c_j A_ij
       exp(i(E_i-E_j)t/hbar).

This is a finite trigonometric polynomial. It cannot equal

  C exp(-gamma t)  (C !=0, gamma>0)

for every t>=0: the latter converges to zero, whereas a finite
trigonometric polynomial with such a limit must be identically zero.

Therefore a finite closed two-electron Hamiltonian with a fixed
bounded current-energy readout cannot EXACTLY reproduce the nonzero
NSE viscous exponential-energy law for all t>=0.

Separately, if one identifies

  E_NSE[u(t)] = <Psi_t,H Psi_t>

with a time-independent self-adjoint total quantum Hamiltonian H,
the right-hand side is constant while E_NSE[u(t)] strictly decreases
for any nonconstant mean-zero viscous NSE velocity. The proposed
identity then contradicts the dissipation law. A time-dependent drive,
an environment/reservoir, a local subsystem energy, or another
nonconserved readout is necessary for a full physical bridge.

This no-go does NOT prevent oscillatory/decaying subsystem observables
for infinite-dimensional quantum systems with continuous spectra
and suitable limits; it identifies the limits of the frozen finite
closed two-electron truncation.
