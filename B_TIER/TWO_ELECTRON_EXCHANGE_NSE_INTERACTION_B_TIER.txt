SCOPE
-----
Two normalized and orthogonal Pauli orbitals are antisymmetrized into a
two-electron wavefunction. Their one-body probability current is projected to
an NSE velocity field. Unlike a single helix, two transverse helices with
different wave numbers generate nonzero solenoidal quadratic interactions.
Nevertheless the initial field is independent of y and its NSE solution
remains in the globally regular 2D3C class. A distinct periodic-background
Coulomb exchange expectation gives a finite interaction-energy diagnostic.

NO CLAIM IS MADE that the full two-electron Coulomb/Maxwell evolution equals
the NSE trajectory. An NSE bilinear interaction is not a physical electron
pair interaction without a dynamical identification.

0. PARAMETERS
-------------
L>0, V=L^3, nu>0, hbar>0, m_e>0;
n,m,r in Z\{0};
k=2 pi n/L, ell=2 pi m/L, q=2 pi r/L;
alpha,beta in R.
Spatial coordinates (x,y,z) on T_L^3.
theta=kz+alpha; phi=ell x+beta.

All L2 norms are unnormalized physical integrals over T_L^3.

1. NORMALIZED PAULI ORBITALS
---------------------------
Define

  psi_1(x,y,z)
    = 1/sqrt(2V) * (1, exp(i theta))^T,

  psi_2(x,y,z)
    = exp(i q y) exp(i phi/2)/sqrt(V)
      * (cos(pi/4-phi/2), i sin(pi/4-phi/2))^T.

The second spinor is L-periodic in x: shifting phi by an integer multiple of
2 pi changes the signs of exp(i phi/2) and both trigonometric factors, leaving
their products invariant.

Both densities are constant:

  psi_1^dagger psi_1=psi_2^dagger psi_2=1/V.

Hence ||psi_1||_2=||psi_2||_2=1.

Because q != 0, psi_2 carries a nontrivial exp(i q y) factor while psi_1
does not. Integration in y gives

  <psi_1,psi_2>=0.

The correctly antisymmetrized two-electron wavefunction is therefore

  Psi_12(X_1,X_2)
    = [psi_1(X_1) tensor psi_2(X_2)
       - psi_2(X_1) tensor psi_1(X_2)]/sqrt(2),

where each X_j includes position and spin, and ||Psi_12||=1.

This state obeys Fermi antisymmetry. Its total one-particle density
integrates to 2, not 1.

2. SPIN TEXTURES AND PROBABILITY CURRENTS
-----------------------------------------
The normalized Pauli spin vectors are

  s_1=(cos theta,sin theta,0),
  s_2=(0,cos phi,sin phi).

They satisfy

  curl s_1 = -k s_1,
  curl s_2 = -ell s_2,
  div s_1 = div s_2=0,
  mean s_1=mean s_2=0.

For A=0 the Dirac-derived full Pauli probability current is

  j[psi]
    = (hbar/m_e) Im(psi^dagger grad psi)
      + (hbar/(2m_e)) curl(psi^dagger sigma psi).

Define U_k=hbar k/(2m_e), U_ell=hbar ell/(2m_e).

The normalized single-electron current velocities are

  v_1=j[psi_1]/(1/V)=U_k e_z-U_k s_1,

  v_2=j[psi_2]/(1/V)
     =U_ell e_x+(hbar q/m_e)e_y-U_ell s_2.

For the antisymmetrized two-electron state the one-body number density and
current are sums of the orbital expectations:

  n_12=2/V,
  j_12=(1/V)(v_1+v_2).

Therefore the average electron current velocity is

  v_12=j_12/n_12=(v_1+v_2)/2.

Let P_{sigma,0} denote mean-zero Leray projection. The two-electron NSE datum is

  a_12:=P_{sigma,0} v_12
       =A s_1 + B s_2,

  A=-U_k/2=-hbar k/(4m_e),
  B=-U_ell/2=-hbar ell/(4m_e).

The 1/2 factors follow from dividing the total number current by the
two-particle number density. Summing raw velocities without those factors
would define a different adapter.

3. QUADRATIC CROSS-INTERACTION
------------------------------
Since each helix separately depends only on its transverse axis,

  (s_1 dot grad)s_1=0,
  (s_2 dot grad)s_2=0.

But for a=a_12=A s_1+B s_2,

  f:=(a dot grad)a
    = AB * (
        -k sin(theta) sin(phi),
        (k-ell) cos(theta) sin(phi),
        ell cos(theta) cos(phi)
      ).

The cross term is not identically zero whenever AB != 0.

To isolate the physically relevant incompressible nonlinearity, solve

  Delta p_0=-div f.

Indeed

  div f=-2 AB k ell sin(theta) cos(phi),

and one periodic mean-zero pressure is

  p_0
    =-2AB k ell/(k^2+ell^2)
      * sin(theta) cos(phi).

The solenoidal quadratic interaction is

  P_sigma f=f+grad p_0

    =AB * (
       k (ell^2-k^2)/(k^2+ell^2) sin(theta) sin(phi),
       (k-ell) cos(theta) sin(phi),
       ell (ell^2-k^2)/(k^2+ell^2) cos(theta) cos(phi)
      ).

For nonzero A,B:

  P_sigma f=0  iff  k=ell.

Its exact squared L2 magnitude is

  ||P_sigma f||_2^2
    = V A^2 B^2/4 *
      [ (ell^2-k^2)^2/(k^2+ell^2) + (k-ell)^2 ].

Thus k != ell gives a genuinely nonzero NSE nonlinear coupling and
excites spatial wave numbers (plus/minus ell,0,plus/minus k).

This is an NSE interaction diagnostic, NOT a Coulomb-force identity.

4. TWO DISTINCT GLOBAL NSE REGULARITY CASES
--------------------------------------------

CASE I: k=ell.

The sum is a Beltrami field:

  curl a=-k a,
  Delta a=-k^2 a,

  (a dot grad)a=grad(|a|^2/2).

Therefore for any nu>0

  u(t)=exp(-nu k^2 t) a,

  p(t)=-exp(-2nu k^2 t)|a|^2/2 + constant

is an explicit globally smooth solution of unforced 3D NSE on T_L^3.

CASE II: k != ell.

Here P_sigma[(a dot grad)a] != 0: the two heat-decaying helices do not remain
a closed two-mode solution. Nevertheless a is independent of y:

  partial_y a=0.

The unforced 3D NSE equations preserve this symmetry. Write

  w=(u_x,u_z),  b=u_y,  nabla_2=(partial_x,partial_z).

Then the exact reduced equations are

  partial_t w+(w dot nabla_2)w+nabla_2 p=nu Delta_2 w,
  div_2 w=0,

  partial_t b+(w dot nabla_2)b=nu Delta_2 b.

The planar velocity w solves global 2D Navier-Stokes, independently of b;
b solves a globally regular passive advection-diffusion equation.
For smooth periodic initial data this yields a globally smooth 2D3C
solution, and weak-strong uniqueness identifies it with every Leray-Hopf
3D solution having this same initial datum.

THUS FOR BOTH CASES

  u in C^infty([0,infinity) x T_L^3),
  Sing_t(u)=empty,
  T_actual_regularity_gap=0.

There is no amplitude smallness restriction on this 2D3C NSE result.

5. QFB CERTIFICATE FOR THE TWO-ELECTRON DATUM
---------------------------------------------
Orthogonality of the spatial patterns under torus integration gives

  integral s_1 dot s_2 dx=0.

Consequently,

  ||a_12||_2^2
    =V(A^2+B^2)
    =V hbar^2(k^2+ell^2)/(16m_e^2).

The frozen periodic QFB time is

  T_QFB(a_12,nu)
    =||a_12||_2^4/(8pi^2 nu^5)

    =L^6 hbar^4(k^2+ell^2)^2
      /(2048 pi^2 m_e^4 nu^5).

It is positive for nonzero k,ell, despite the already-proved globally smooth
NSE evolution. Thus the general QFB certificate remains conservative.

For Gaussian regularization a_epsilon=exp((epsilon^2/4)Delta)a_12, replace

  A -> A_epsilon=A exp(-epsilon^2 k^2/4),
  B -> B_epsilon=B exp(-epsilon^2 ell^2/4).

Then

  ||a_epsilon||_2^2
    =V hbar^2/(16m_e^2) *
      (k^2 exp(-epsilon^2k^2/2)
       +ell^2 exp(-epsilon^2ell^2/2)).

The same regularity and interaction conclusions remain valid, with the
filtered amplitudes.

6. ACTUAL ELECTRON-ELECTRON COULOMB/EXCHANGE INTERACTION
-------------------------------------------------------
An NSE cross-nonlinearity is not an electromagnetic repulsion term. To define
the two-electron Coulomb model on a compact torus with total charge -2e,
include a neutralizing +2e background and let G_L be the zero-mean periodic
Coulomb Green function:

  -Delta G_L=delta_0-1/V,
  integral G_L dx=0.

The background-subtracted pair potential is

  V_ee(x_1-x_2)=(e^2/epsilon_0) G_L(x_1-x_2).

The initial two-electron expectation is

  E_ee
    = <Psi_12, V_ee Psi_12>
    = E_direct - E_exchange.

Because each orbital density equals 1/V, the direct Hartree term vanishes
under this exact zero-mean Green convention:

  E_direct=0.

Set f(x)=psi_1(x)^dagger psi_2(x).
The orbital-overlap function has four Fourier modes:

  f(x)
   =e^{iqy}/(2sqrt(2)V) *
     [ exp(i pi/4)(1+exp(-i theta))
       +exp(-i pi/4) exp(i phi)(1-exp(-i theta)) ].

The four wave vectors are

  (0,q,0), (0,q,-k), (ell,q,0), (ell,q,-k).

Using F(p)=integral_{T_L^3} f(x) exp(-i p dot x) dx,
each nonzero Fourier coefficient has |F(p)|^2=1/8.

Since

  -Delta G_L=delta_0-1/V,

the Fourier representation gives

  E_exchange
    = e^2/(epsilon_0 V) sum_{p != 0} |F(p)|^2/|p|^2

    =e^2/(8 epsilon_0 V) *
     [ 1/q^2
       +1/(q^2+k^2)
       +1/(q^2+ell^2)
       +1/(q^2+k^2+ell^2) ].

Hence the EXACT total pair-interaction expectation in this
background-subtracted periodic Green convention is

  E_ee
    =-e^2/(8 epsilon_0 L^3) *
      [ 1/q^2
        +1/(q^2+k^2)
        +1/(q^2+ell^2)
        +1/(q^2+k^2+ell^2) ].

IMPORTANT PHYSICAL SIGN CONVENTION:
This is an energy relative to the imposed neutralizing background and the
zero-spatial-mean periodic potential. Its negative sign is an exchange
contribution RELATIVE TO THAT CHOSEN REFERENCE. It does not assert an
attractive bare Coulomb force between two electrons; changing the electrostatic
reference or removing the background alters the physical interpretation.

This is an exact initial-state expectation, not a two-electron eigenvalue or
a proof that the chosen Slater determinant is invariant under interacting
two-electron Schrödinger/Pauli evolution.

Minimal input:

  physical constants hbar,m_e,e,epsilon_0.

Compute

  k=2pi n/L, ell=2pi m/L, q=2pi r/L,
  A=-hbar k/(4m_e), B=-hbar ell/(4m_e).

Outputs:

  R1  two orthogonal normalized one-electron Pauli orbitals;
  R2  normalized antisymmetrized two-electron Slater determinant;
  R3  mean current velocity and mean-zero NSE datum;
  R4  exact NSE nonlinear solenoidal interaction P_sigma f;
  R5  exact norm ||P_sigma f||_2;
  R6  exact QFB certificate T_QFB;
  R7  globally regular NSE class, including k != ell;
  R8  exact background-subtracted Coulomb exchange energy E_ee;
  R9  physical calibration candidates, kept distinct from identities.

Representative comparison choices:

  - n=m=1, r=1: closed Beltrami NSE, P_sigma f=0;
  - n=1, m=2, r=1: genuine nonlinear NSE interaction, 2D3C global;
  - n=1, m=-1, r=1: nonzero cross NSE interaction even though
    k^2=ell^2; equal Laplacian eigenvalues alone do not suffice for Beltrami.

A computational time scale hbar/|E_ee| can be assigned if E_ee !=0, but it is
a convention, not a rigorous interaction-evolution or uncertainty time.

CONCLUSION
----------
Two electron-current profiles can interact nonlinearly through NSE, while
remaining globally regular because of 2D3C symmetry. Separately their
fermionic state possesses a calculable periodic Coulomb exchange expectation.
These supply distinct, testable mathematical endpoints; neither identifies
NSE interaction with physical electron-electron coupling.
